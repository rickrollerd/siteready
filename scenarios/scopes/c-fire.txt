1. SCOPE OF WORKS 
Notice to Tenderers
Tenderers are to submit their pricing schedule in the prescribed format provided by the builder with this Tender. 
Tenderers must conduct a review of the documentation and provide a list of potential Value Engineering opportunities. This must include a detail overview of the Value Engineering initiatives, along with the benefits and proposed savings. Input into the constructability of the design and any potential issues that may occur in the built form is also to be provided.
In circumstances where the Tenderer provides engineering input and value management into the design, the Tenderer is to allow for any associated change in design which impacts the Scope of Works. The Tenderer shall also advise the builder of any related impact on other contractors.
Tenderers shall ensure they have the relevant licenced and accredited persons within their Company to complete the Scope of Works detailed below and provide copies of certifications and/or licences with their Tender. 
Where Tenderers intend to secondary subcontract any part of their works, they must identify any proposed secondary subcontractors, along with the secondary subcontractor’s scope of works, within their Tender. No works are to be secondary subcontracted without the prior written approval of the builder.

1.1 GENERAL
The Scope of Works comprises the Fire Services to the project as defined and in accordance with the requirements of:
(a) This Scope of Works;
(b) Subcontract Documents;
(c) The Subcontract and Schedules.
Unless specifically excluded herein.
Unless noted otherwise, the Scope of Works is to allow for the provision of all preliminaries, labour (1 labour assistant person per 14 trades personnel, or part thereof), supervision, materials, tools, plant, equipment, insurances and sundry items for the supply, documentation (e.g. shop drawings, Operation & Maintenance manuals (O&M manuals), QA etc.), design finalisation, installation, manufacture, transport and satisfactory completion of the Subcontract Works in accordance with the Subcontract Documents, all relevant statutory codes, codes of practice, Australian Standards and the National Construction Code (NCC).
For avoidance of doubt, the Scope of Works includes all items described in the Fire Services Specifications as well as items listed, nominated, implied and/or shown on the Drawings and Other Documents relating to the Works unless specifically excluded herein. 
All requirements of the Subcontract Documents, including but not limited to the Drawings and Specifications, are included in the Subcontractor Sum, regardless of whether specifically identified in the pricing schedule, unless specifically identified as an exclusion.
References to the “Fire Subcontractor” or “services subcontractors” contained within this Scope of Works are to be interpreted as references to the Subcontractor.
References to the “Tenderer”, “Contractor”, “Builder” or the like contained in the Subcontract Documents, for the purposes of the Works, are to be interpreted as references to the Subcontractor.
In certain instances, there will be more than one subcontractor assigned to a Trade Package and as such, the builder reserves the right to add or remove to or from the Scope of Works of any subcontract package at its discretion.

3.10 Drilling, Coring and Cutting of Concrete
Drilling, Coring and Cutting of the building structure has the potential to impact both the building structural integrity and cast in services pathways (conduits). In order to ensure this does not occur, the Subcontractor is to work in accordance with the Cutting Drilling and Core Holing Procedure, which involves the use of a Cutting Drilling and Core Holing Permit.
Following is a summary of the Cutting Drilling and Core Holing Procedure, however the Subcontractor must ensure it is familiar with the full procedure which is located within the Subcontractor HSE Pack. Should the Subcontractor fail to work in accordance with the Cutting Drilling and Core Holing Procedure, and this results in damage to conduits or cables, the Subcontractor will be back charged for the costs incurred in identifying and rectifying the damage. 
Should the Subcontractor fail to follow the process summarised at 3.10.1 below prior to the energisation of Buildings, they will be required to follow the process summarised at 3.10.2 for all Buildings at both stages.
3.10.1 Prior to Energization of Buildings
Prior to the energisation of a Building, the following process applies to Drilling, Coring and Cutting within that Building;
(a) Task Risk Assessments (TRAs) are to be updated and require the use of a concrete scanning device that has the ability to detect cast in conduits, such as a proprietary concrete scanning detection device;
(b) Drilling, coring and cutting can occur once the area to be penetrated is scanned and deemed free of cast in conduits;
(c) The Subcontractor must nominate a responsible competent person to manage the task, that is trained in the use of the scanning device;
(d) the builder are to clearly delineate temporary “Live” cast in-situ services, TRAs are to consider cast in services and deal with the delineated zones. These zones are “exclusion zones” for penetrations. Prior to any drilling, coring and cutting in an exclusion zone, the Subcontractor must complete the current Cutting Drilling and Core Holing Permit and gain approval from the builder; and
(e) the builder will ensure that cast in temporary services are only present in in-ground slabs, all other temporary electrical services will be surface mounted and highlighted via the use of temporary electrical tagging i.e yellow tape.
3.10.2 Post Energization of Buildings
Upon energisation of a Building, the following process applies to that Building;
(a) the builder will communicate to all subcontractors when a building is about to be energised. This will typically align with commissioning of the Building, i.e once most penetrations have occurred;
(b) the builder will advertise the energized state of a building at each main entry point to the building and via regular updates at pre-start meetings and project notice boards; and
(c) The Subcontractor must complete the Cutting Drilling and Core Holing Permit for all penetrations.

4.11  Witnessing of Testing and Commissioning Activities
5.1.1	Levels for Testing and Commissioning
There are four levels for testing and commissioning (which are reflected into the Construction Programme):
(a) Infrastructure – This is all works external to individual new buildings to be constructed as part of this Project. Infrastructure to support buildings must be in place and operational before any Building can be successfully tested and commissioned;
(b) Building – Each building is to be tested in isolation. Testing is to also prove successful interconnection into infrastructure including BMS, chilled water, fire panel, water supply, power supply, communications interconnectivity, security systems interconnectivity. It is also to verify load shedding operations via simulated ESN signals. Water Management System operates as per design. (Existing) fire pump operates when required;
(c) Neighbourhood – when all buildings forming part of a neighbourhood have been individually tested and commissioned, the operation of the neighbourhood is to be tested.  There are three main Communications rooms on site – in buildings GH, E1 and E2. Neighbourhoods are generally defined as a group of buildings with a common engineering systems connection. Neighbourhood testing is to prove all testing relating to that group of buildings operates as expected. This will require limited retesting within individual buildings. Examples include but are not limited to; 
(i) Operation of a smoke detector in SE1 indicates correctly in Entry building E2 fire panel;
(ii) Manual operation of the gas purge switch in E2 activates the fans in the associated building;
(iii) Building Management System monitoring and controlling all devices as expected, graphic user interfaces, trending, logging, reporting fully operational as expected;
(iv) Water Management System can be controlled and monitored as per design;
(v) Centralised UPS to be load tested over 4 hours and proven fully operational;  
(vi) Security monitoring/ control in E1 and E2 work as expected; and
(d) Site/ Precinct – This is site wide testing and commissioning and it includes total systems operation such as:
(i) Verification of device (e.g., smoke detector) location;
(ii) Operation of all Communications infrastructure;
(iii) Operation of all Security systems in the Main Control Room (MCR) including all status/ alarm interfaces with other services;
(iv) Operation of generators, including load shedding. Prior to this testing, numerous Engineering Services Network (ESN) load shed simulations with verification of operation of every item of electrical plant during and after a power disruption, including distribution boards, sewer pump stations, fire systems and mechanical plant; 
(v) Operation of all aspects of Water Management System, Energy Metering System, Building Management System and Emergency Lighting System including all Graphic User Interfaces; and
(vi) Site/ precinct testing is to prove all testing relating to the Site operates as expected.  As per the neighbourhood testing, this will require limited retesting within individual buildings to prove functionality.   
5.1.2	Witness Testing Requirements
For each of the above levels of testing, there are witness testing requirements for all elements as follows:
(a) Subcontractor to carry out all testing and commissioning to ensure system integrity and operations are as required.  the builder will be present during this process.  All results are to be formalised and submitted to the builder via the ITP process;
(b) Witness Testing by the builder – If the builder did not fully witness the commissioning testing by the Subcontractor or is not satisfied that this witnessing was sufficient, this subcontractor is to allow for dedicated witness commissioning testing by the builder. This will generally be random testing verifying the results within the ITPs, but this testing may require a 100% retest for selected tests. For example, fire matrix testing;
(c) Witness Testing by the consultant – The Subcontractor is to allow for dedicated witness commissioning testing by the consultant. This will generally be random testing verifying the results within the ITPs, but this testing may require a 100% retest for selected tests. For example, fire matrix testing;
(d) Witness testing by Statutory Authorities – If there is a Statutory Authority with jurisdiction over the works, the Subcontractor is required to allow for whatever commissioning testing is requested to be witnessed and proven. For all such commissioning testing with the fire authority, the Subcontractor can expect the builder, the consultant, the Principal, the Building Certifier and the Fire Engineer to be invited to attend and be present;
(e) Witness Testing by the Principal and Project Authorities – The Subcontractor is to allow for dedicated witness commissioning testing by the Principal and by Project Authorities. Authorities in this case refers to the Building Certifier and the Fire Engineer. This will generally be random testing verifying the results within the ITPs, but this testing may require a 100% retest for selected tests.  For example, fire matrix testing; and
(f) The Subcontractor is to provide a matrix indicating all items to be tested/ commissioned plus is to clearly indicate all the above witness testing types.  the builder, in conjunction with the Subcontractor will then add to this list as required and pre-fill witness expectations for the consultant, the Principal and Project Authorities. This listing/ matrix will then be forwarded to the consultant, the Principal and Project Authorities for their consideration and advice regarding requirements. It can be expected that additional tests will be added, especially for integrated systems checks.

4.13 Interim Measures to Ensure Integrity of Plant and Systems is Maintained
Once a building has been successfully tested and commissioned, it will be locked and entry restricted.  A log book will be kept at the entry to each building for recording who entered, date, reason and works carried out. 

The Subcontractor’s Commissioning and Handover Management Plan and scope is to include interim measures to ensure integrity of plant and systems between the time a building is tested and commissioned and Practical Completion.  Such items will include:
(a) Installation of covers over all smoke detectors after commissioning and permanent removal just prior to Practical Completion;
(b) Installation of temporary construction filter media in all air handling plant prior to energisation and permanent removal just prior to Practical Completion.  In the interim, filter media is to be cleaned/ replaced as required;
(c) Scope for flushing pans, potable water, cleaning/ replacing filters on ZIPs etc will all be as per the Potable Water Reticulation Management Handbook (which forms part of the Hydraulics subcontract);
(d) Daily inspections of each communications room, UPS room, MSB room, substation to ensure no unauthorised access, rooms are locked, clean and conditions are being maintained;
(e) Maintain all plant and systems as required to ensure that integrity of operation is maintained.  For plant such as the chillers, this will include chiller maintenance as per manufacturer’s recommendations.  For emergency lighting, this will include rectification of any automatic system/ device problem report; and
(f) Cleaning: In addition to the building cleaning, the associated services trade will be responsible for the following specialist cleaning with a final clean just before Practical Completion;
(i) Cleaning/ replacing filters as required.  This includes any filters within any switchboard/ panel or hydraulic item;
(ii) Thorough clean inside all main plant including air handling units, electrical switchboards, mechanical switchboards, Fire Panels, Security panels;
(iii) Clean sensors and items within plant and ductwork such as MASD sampling points;
(iv) The Communications subcontractor to clean inside all racks and is to be responsible for cleaning requirements within all SER rooms/ cupboards.  This scope is to take effect from when the associated SER room/ cupboard is handed over to the Communications subcontractor for installation of racks to commence; and
(v) The Electrical subcontractor will be responsible for all cleaning requirements within high voltage rooms, main switchboard rooms and UPS rooms, once the associated equipment is energised.
